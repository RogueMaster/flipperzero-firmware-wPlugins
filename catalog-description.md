Handpan Chords turns your Flipper into a chord reference and practice tool for
handpans.

Pick your drum, scroll through every chord it can actually play, and see exactly
which pads to strike on a diagram of the shell. Practice mode plays melodic
phrases back one strike at a time so you can follow along and learn them.

## Drums

Nine 9-note handpans are built in: D Kurd 9, D Celtic Minor 9, D Sabye 9,
C# Amara 9, E La Sirena 9, B Kurd 9, A Kurd 9, F Low Pygmy 9 and C Major 9.

## Chords

Nothing is hardcoded per drum. Every chord is worked out at runtime from the
drum's tuning, so only chords the instrument can genuinely play are offered:
41 of them on D Kurd 9, 12 on F Low Pygmy 9.

A filled pad on the diagram means strike it, an outline means leave it. When a
note sits on two pads, both light up, so you can pick whichever voicing falls
under your hands.

Set the chord depth to taste: triads only, plus sixths and sevenths, or
everything including ninths and elevenths.

## Practice

Fourteen phrases, from a simple walk up to the fifth through to arpeggiated
chord progressions. Each one is transposed and voiced for whichever drum you
have selected, and a phrase that needs a note your drum lacks is simply not
offered.

The pad to strike is filled and the one coming next is marked with a dot. Play
it back at Slow, Medium or Fast, or step through it a note at a time at your own
pace. Some phrases use the ding as a bass voice, and some ask for two pads to be
struck together.

## Favorites

Star any chord and it is saved to the SD card, ready the next time you open the
app.

## Controls

In the chord view, Up and Down move through the chords, Left and Right change
drum, and OK saves a favorite.

In practice mode, Up and Down set the speed, Left and Right step through the
phrase by hand, and OK plays it back.

Back always returns to where you came from.

## Adding your own drum

The app is open source. Because chords and phrases are derived rather than
listed, adding a handpan takes one array of note numbers and one row in the
scale table, and every chord and compatible practice phrase appears for it
automatically. See the project page for a worked example.
